Who Is Gimli?

Gimli, son of Glóin, was a Dwarf of the House of Durin and a member of the Fellowship of the Ring. Unlike other Dwarves of his time, he readily fought alongside Elves in the War of the Ring against Sauron. After Sauron's defeat, he was given lordship over the Glittering Caves at Helm's Deep, which he presided over until he departed from Middle-earth.

"Faithless is he that says farewell when the road darkens."

Heritage

Born in TA 2879, Gimli was the son of Glóin and a nephew of Óin, both former companions of Bilbo Baggins on the Quest of Erebor. He was a descendant of Durin the Deathless, father of the first house of the Dwarven people. Through his father, Gimli was also the first cousin once removed of Balin, self-proclaimed Lord of Moria. When Thorin and Company set out on the Quest of Erebor, young Gimli was prevented from joining because they thought him too young — though Gimli, being in his sixties, considered himself ripe for adventure and was deeply disappointed.

The Fellowship

Gimli represented the Dwarves in the Fellowship of the Ring, chosen at the Council of Elrond to accompany Frodo on his quest to destroy the One Ring. Throughout the journey, he proved himself a fierce and loyal companion. His initial mistrust of the Elves, particularly Legolas of the Woodland Realm, transformed into one of the most celebrated friendships in Middle-earth — a bond that would endure beyond the end of the Third Age.

Abilities

As a Dwarf, Gimli enjoyed the great brawn and stamina typical of his race. He could carry the weight of his armour as if it were nothing, and together with Aragorn and Legolas, he ran 45 leagues in less than four days with only a few hours of sleep. He was a powerful warrior and capable axe-wielder who survived innumerable confrontations with Sauron's forces, including the battles at Helm's Deep, Pelennor Fields, and the Black Gate of Mordor.

The Glittering Caves

After the War of the Ring, Gimli became Lord of the Glittering Caves at Helm's Deep, where he led a colony of Dwarves who did great works there. Like the Dwarves who carved the great halls of Moria — solid, functional, enduring — Gimli valued craftsmanship above all things. He is said to have eventually sailed to the Undying Lands with Legolas, making him the only Dwarf ever to do so.
