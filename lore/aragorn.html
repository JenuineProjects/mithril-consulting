Who Is Aragorn?

Aragorn II, son of Arathorn II, also known as Strider and later King Elessar, was the 16th and last Chieftain of the Dúnedain of the North and the first High King of the Reunited Kingdom of Gondor and Arnor since the days of Isildur. He was a great Ranger and warrior, and as Isildur's heir, he bore the shards of Narsil, reforged and renamed Andúril — "Flame of the West" — in the War of the Ring.

"I am Aragorn son of Arathorn, and am called Elessar, the Elfstone, Dúnadan, the heir of Isildur Elendil's son of Gondor. Here is the Sword that was Broken and is forged again!"

Early Life

Aragorn was raised in Rivendell under the care of Elrond after his father Arathorn was killed by Orcs when Aragorn was only two years old. Elrond gave him the name Estel ("Hope") and concealed his true identity until he came of age. It was in Rivendell that Aragorn first met Arwen Undómiel, Elrond's daughter, and they fell in love — a love that would echo the ancient tale of Beren and Lúthien.

The Ranger

Before claiming his kingship, Aragorn spent decades as a Ranger of the North, protecting the Shire and other lands from evil without recognition or thanks. He served in the armies of both Rohan and Gondor under the name Thorongil, winning great victories before disappearing into the wild. Tolkien described him as lean, dark, and tall, with "a shaggy head of dark hair flecked with grey, and in a pale stern face a pair of keen grey eyes."

Leadership

Aragorn did not fight alone. He brought together Elves, Dwarves, Men, and Hobbits — each with different strengths — and directed them toward a shared purpose. His power was not in doing everyone else's work. It was in seeing the whole picture, holding his companions to a standard, and guiding every member of the Fellowship toward the goal.

Powers & Abilities

Aragorn possessed Elven wisdom from his upbringing in Rivendell and the foresight of the Dúnedain. He was a skilled healer, notably with the plant Athelas (Kingsfoil), and a mighty warrior — after the Battle of the Pelennor Fields, he was one of three left unscathed despite fighting in the thick of it. He possessed a near-indomitable will: he once challenged Sauron directly through the Palantír of Orthanc and proved victorious, a feat even Gandalf the White was reluctant to attempt.

He was accounted as "the hardiest of living Men" during his lifetime.
