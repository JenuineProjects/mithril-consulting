Who Is Saruman?

Saruman, also known as Saruman the White, was the first and greatest of the Istari — the Wizards sent by the Valar to Middle-earth in the Third Age to help the Free Peoples counter the possible return of Sauron. He was the head of the White Council and was regarded as the most powerful and knowledgeable of the five Wizards.

"His knowledge was deep, his thought was subtle, and his hands marvellously skilled."

Origin

Before his arrival in Middle-earth, Saruman was a powerful Maia of Aulë the Smith named Curumo. From the beginning he was ambitious and cunning, desiring order above all things. When a council was called by Manwë to send emissaries to Middle-earth, Curumo volunteered and was appointed leader of the group. He arrived at the Grey Havens around the year TA 1000 and became known as Curunír to the Elves, and Saruman to Men.

The White Council

When the White Council was formed around TA 2463, Saruman was appointed its leader, though Galadriel had wanted Gandalf in this position. His deep study of the Rings of Power and the lore of Sauron eventually led him to admire and envy the Dark Lord. Concluding that he should be the one to find the One Ring, Saruman became a traitor at heart, though the rest of the Council remained unaware.

At Isengard

In TA 2759, Saruman settled in the fortress of Orthanc at Isengard. There he discovered one of the remaining palantíri but concealed his find from the White Council. Over time, he fortified Isengard, surrounded himself with Orcs and Dunlendings, and began breeding his own Uruk-hai. Through the palantír, Sauron established contact with Saruman and, daunted by threats and lured by promises of power, the Wizard submitted — though he secretly planned to claim the One Ring for himself.

Powers & Abilities

Saruman was the greatest of the Istari at the peak of his power. He was extremely learned in Ring-lore and the devices of Sauron, knowledge that enabled him to create great forges and explosives. He was capable of breeding his own Uruk-hai and Half-orcs, and commanded many spies.

His greatest power, however, was his voice. Saruman could bend almost any mind to his will simply by speaking. Even with Isengard broken, Gandalf had to be very careful, as Saruman could ensnare nearly anyone with his persuasive speech. Aragorn stated that few other than Gandalf, Elrond, and Galadriel could resist his voice.
